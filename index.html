About

About Pay to Play Politics

Independent coverage of the business of sports and the politics behind it.

Pay to Play Politics covers the intersection of sports business and public power: franchise valuations, stadium deals and public funding, labor, betting regulation, and media rights. The Wire gathers the day’s reporting from Sportico, Front Office Sports, Sports Business Journal, ESPN, Bloomberg, The Athletic, and statehouse reporting nationwide, and every headline links to the original publisher.

The Franchise Ledgers track all 183 teams across the NBA, NFL, MLB, MLS, NHL, WNBA, and NWSL: the controlling owner of each franchise, the year purchased and the price paid, today’s estimated value, and the stadium — what it cost and how it was financed. Values are published estimates from Forbes and Sportico; purchase prices are the reported price of the controlling stake.

The site is published by Chmari L. Anderson. The wire refreshes every morning.

About the numbers & sources

Pay to Play Politics franchise tables were researched and verified. Current values are published estimates, attributed per league: NBA, NFL, MLB and NWSL from Forbes (2026 editions); MLS and WNBA from Sportico (2026); NHL from Forbes (2025, published December 2025 — Sportico published a newer NHL list in September 2026 with higher values). Purchase prices are the reported price of the controlling stake. “Gain” is current estimated value minus purchase price — it excludes debt, operating results, and distributions, so treat it as an appreciation snapshot, not an investment return. Negative gains are real, not errors. Stadium costs are reported original construction costs in nominal dollars at the time of construction; Canadian-dollar costs were converted to USD at approximate historical rates where converted, with originals preserved in the team notes — three Canadian MLS stadium costs are shown as “—” with the CAD figures in the notes. A “—” means no reliable public figure was found; nothing has been filled in or estimated. Pending sales, relocations, and planned stadiums are flagged in each league's team notes.
